# Scrum Methodology & Project Implementation: TrakIt

## 1. Scrum Framework Overview
For the development and testing phases of **TrakIt**, our team will transition from initial Waterfall planning into a **Scrum framework** starting March 2nd. This Agile methodology is structured to deliver the TrakIt prototype through **two-week Sprints**, facilitating the continuous integration of the effort-tracking and risk-management modules.



## 2. Roles and Responsibilities
To ensure accountability across our 6-member team, we have defined the following core Scrum roles:

* **Product Owner (Group Leader):** Responsible for maintaining the Product Backlog, defining requirements for "Key Investment of Time" features, and ensuring the prototype meets all academic evaluation criteria.
* **Scrum Master:** Responsible for facilitating biweekly syncs, removing technical blockers (e.g., Git repository issues), and ensuring the team adheres to the development schedule.
* **Development Team:** A cross-functional group responsible for UI/UX design, backend logic for person-hour calculations, and robust testing of functional requirements.

---

## 3. Sprint Ceremonies
The process is governed by four key events to maintain a regular development cadence:

| Ceremony | TrakIt Context | Frequency |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Sprint Planning** | Selecting specific TrakIt features/user stories for the upcoming interval. | Biweekly |
| **Daily Stand-up** | Brief syncs via Microsoft Teams to identify integration risks or technical hurdles. | Daily (Sync/Async) |
| **Sprint Review** | Internal demonstration of the current TrakIt iteration and feature completion. | End of Sprint |
| **Sprint Retrospective** | Review of group dynamics and adjustments to the development process. | Biweekly |

---

## 4. Rationale & Project Context
We have selected Scrum for the execution phase of TrakIt for the following project-specific reasons:

* **Hybrid Model Alignment:** Our schedule dictates a shift from Waterfall to Agile on 3/2. Scrum provides the necessary structure to transition smoothly from Architectural Design to Active Prototyping.
* **Managing Integration Risk:** As identified in our Risk Assessment, integrating effort tracking with requirements is a high-risk task. Scrum’s incremental delivery ensures we test these connections early and often.
* **Granular Visibility:** The core mission of TrakIt is "Key Investment of Time." By using Scrum, the team practices what we preach—tracking our own person-hours across Sprints to ensure alignment with project goals.
* **Scope Control:** To prevent Scope Creep, the Scrum framework requires that any changes to the backlog are documented and approved, ensuring we stay within the **Minimum Working Prototype** constraints for the 4/26 deadline.
